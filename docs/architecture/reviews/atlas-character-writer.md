# Atlas Character Writer — Review

Scope per `.claude/agents/atlas-character-writer.md`: campaign biographies for leaders/civilizations, grounded in the existing leader-profile system (`civ_identity_ambitions_specification.md`) and the World Chronicle's narrative-template pattern (`world_chronicle_specification.md`, `emergent_crisis_history_specification.md`). This review does not propose new trait/weakness/ambition/fear vocabulary, a new civilization data structure, or new-game-flow screens — those are `atlas-civilization-designer` and `atlas-ui-ux-designer` territory.

I read `atlas-game-director.md` before writing this. Its Finding 5 and Recommendation 4 already establish the operating constraint I'm designing against: a campaign biography is a template/render layer over the existing Leader Profile schema, structurally identical to the Chronicle's `compileWarRecord`-style pattern (`world_chronicle_specification.md` §3) applied to a Leader Profile object instead of a Chronicle event. This review adopts that framing rather than re-litigating it, and focuses on the concrete templates and the ownership split the director flagged as unresolved (its Contradiction #3 and Open Question #4).

## Campaign Biography Concept

A campaign biography is a short (roughly 3–6 sentence) prose passage shown at civilization/leader selection and at new-game confirmation, generated by reading the **existing** fields already defined in `civ_identity_ambitions_specification.md` §2 — 2 Major Traits, 1 Weakness, 1 Governing Priority, 1 Diplomatic Tendency, 1 Military Tendency, 1 Personal Ambition, 1 Fear — plus the assigned Long-Term Ambition (§3), and rendering them through a fixed prose template. It is not a new data model: there is no "biography" object with its own fields. The renderer takes a `LeaderProfile` (already fully specified) as its only mechanical input, exactly the way `compileWarRecord(entry: ChronicleEntry)` takes a `ChronicleEntry` as its only input.

This keeps biography text mechanically honest: every sentence in a biography must be traceable to a field the AI/utility engine actually uses, so the biography never promises player-facing behavior the simulation doesn't implement. That traceability requirement is the same discipline the Chronicle's war-naming grammar already applies to `causality.trigger_type` and `causality.resource` (`emergent_crisis_history_specification.md` §3A) — no adjective appears in output text unless it maps to a real input field.

Proposed render function shape, matching the Chronicle's own style (`world_chronicle_specification.md` §3):

```typescript
function compileLeaderBiography(profile: LeaderProfile, civ: CivilizationIdentity): string {
  const template = `
${civ.name}, led by ${profile.name}.

${renderTraitClause(profile.majorTraits)}. ${renderWeaknessClause(profile.weakness)}.
As ${profile.leaderTitle ?? "ruler"}, ${profile.name} has committed the state to ${renderPriorityClause(profile.governingPriority)}.
${renderAmbitionFearClause(profile.personalAmbition, profile.fear)}
${renderLongTermAmbitionClause(profile.longTermAmbition)}
`;
  return template;
}
```

Each `render*Clause` helper is a lookup table keyed on the enumerated option (e.g. one prose fragment per Major Trait, one per Weakness), not a generative model — consistent with the Chronicle's grammar-based concatenation rather than freeform generation.

## Preset Examples

`atlas-civilization-designer`'s preset proposal was not yet available in `docs/architecture/reviews/` at the time this was written (see Contradictions section below), so these examples use plausible presets built directly from the trait/weakness/priority/ambition/fear vocabulary already enumerated in `civ_identity_ambitions_specification.md` §2, to give the Project Director and other specialists concrete text to react to on tone and length. They should be treated as illustrative, not final, until reconciled with the actual preset list.

**Example 1 — Militaristic/Expansionist preset, "Valgardia":**
> Valgardia, led by Konrad Ironhand.
> A militaristic and expansionist ruler, Konrad has built his reign on conquest — his armies expand faster and hit harder than his neighbors', but his war councils are famously indecisive once a campaign begins, delaying the order to strike until the moment has often already passed.
> As ruler, Konrad has committed the state to Armed Forces Expansion, filling the treasury's Barracks queue before any granary.
> He dreams of uniting the continent under one banner, and fears above all being hemmed in — every foreign border drawn near his own reads to him as an act of war waiting to happen.
> His long-term ambition is to unite the continent by conquest.

**Example 2 — Mercantile/Diplomatic preset, "Osteria":**
> Osteria, led by Queen Isolde Farrow.
> Isolde governs through trade and treaty rather than the sword — her diplomats close deals other courts wouldn't dare propose, and her merchants set the prices her rivals are forced to match. Her economic instincts falter, however, when the ledgers turn red: upkeep goes unquestioned even as the treasury empties.
> As ruler, she has committed the state to Treasury Maximization, taxing generously to keep the coffers full.
> She has set her sights on becoming the continent's wealthiest merchant house, and privately fears starvation more than war — the granaries are never allowed to run low, whatever else goes short.
> Her long-term ambition is to command the greatest trading wealth on the continent.

Both examples are five sentences, one paragraph each, no invented facts (no birthplace, no dynasty, no age) — because those fields don't exist in the current schema. Tone is grand-strategy flavor text (comparable register to Civilization's leader blurbs), not literary fiction.

## Custom/Procedural Generation Approach

For AI leaders (always fully procedural per `mvp_specification.md` §3.3: "2 Personality Traits, 1 Weakness, 1 Governing Priority, 1 Diplomatic Tendency, 1 Military Tendency, 1 Personal Ambition, and 1 Fear") the biography renderer above runs unmodified — every slot is filled, so every clause resolves.

For the **player's own leader**, `mvp_specification.md` §3.3 currently only has the player choose a name and two personality traits; nothing in the spec has the player choose a Weakness, Governing Priority, Diplomatic/Military Tendency, Ambition, or Fear. That means as the new-game flow is currently specified, the player-leader object is a **partial** `LeaderProfile`, and `compileLeaderBiography` cannot resolve every clause. Two ways this can be handled without inventing new data:

1. **Backfill via existing procedural generation.** Auto-assign the unspecified slots (Weakness, Priority, Tendencies, Ambition, Fear) the same way AI profiles are generated, using the same seed/RNG, and show the player the resulting biography as a preview at leader-selection time (possibly with a "reroll" affordance). This keeps the schema untouched and gives a full biography immediately.
2. **Degrade gracefully.** Render only the clauses backed by fields the player actually chose (name + 2 traits) and omit clauses for unset fields, rather than fabricating placeholder text. This is a template-engineering choice (conditional clause omission), not a new data model — the Chronicle template already does this for optional fields (`${entry.secondary_actors.length > 0 ? ... : ''}` in `world_chronicle_specification.md` §3).

I recommend option 1 (backfill) for biography completeness, but flag this as a decision for the Project Director since it touches new-game-flow sequencing (does biography preview happen before or after the "hidden" fields are rolled?) — that is `atlas-ui-ux-designer`/`atlas-civilization-designer` territory, not mine to resolve unilaterally.

For **fully custom leaders** (if `atlas-civilization-designer` proposes a mode where the player hand-picks every slot, not just 2 traits) — see the ownership split below.

## LLM vs. Template Boundary

`game_design_document.md` §1 states LLM integration (via Ollama/Llama.cpp) is an **Early Access** feature, not MVP. Neither the GDD nor the specifications read for this review state a narrower per-feature LLM boundary in their own text (the "diplomacy wording, leader dialogue, historical summaries" phrasing given in my role brief describes the intended eventual LLM use cases, but I did not find it as a direct quote in the documents I was asked to ground against — flagging this rather than presenting it as sourced).

Applying the GDD's stage boundary directly: for MVP, campaign biographies must be 100% template-based — the `compileLeaderBiography` lookup-table approach above, with zero runtime LLM calls, exactly like the Chronicle's war/era naming is deterministic string concatenation, not generation. This also matches the project's determinism requirement (`game_design_document.md` §7: full PRNG state serialization for save/load) — an LLM call would break save-state determinism unless its output were cached and serialized alongside the profile, which is out of scope for MVP.

Post-MVP (Early Access), the same template system is the natural place to *later* substitute an LLM call that takes the same structured `LeaderProfile` input and produces richer prose, without changing the interface other systems depend on (`biography: string` in, `LeaderProfile` out) — mirroring the `WorldRenderer` interface precedent `atlas-game-director` cites for the renderer swap. That substitution should be scoped as an EA-stage initiative, not built now.

## Contradictions or Disagreements Found

**1. Overlap with `atlas-civilization-designer` on custom-leader biographies (per Project Director's brief).** `MULTI_AGENT_WORKFLOW.md` assigns `atlas-civilization-designer` "civilization selection, custom creation, civilization data structure" and assigns me "campaign biographies grounded in the existing leader-profile/Chronicle systems." `atlas-game-director.md` (Contradiction #3) flagged that these two roles "may disagree on whether a fully player-customized civilization/leader should still receive a procedurally-generated biography, or whether custom leaders opt out of procedural narrative entirely."

I am proposing the following explicit division so this doesn't become a fought-over seam: **`atlas-civilization-designer` owns which trait/weakness/ambition/fear fields exist and which combinations a custom-creation flow allows a player to pick** (the data contract — what slots exist, what values are legal, whether new slots like "dynasty name" or "birthplace" get added to the schema). **I own the text/template generation layer that consumes whatever profile object civilization-designer's data contract produces** — i.e., `compileLeaderBiography` above is written against the *shape* of `LeaderProfile`, not against a fixed enumeration of presets, so it should keep working unmodified as long as new custom fields are additive (new optional slots) rather than restructuring existing ones. If civilization-designer's custom-creation flow allows partially-filled profiles (analogous to the player-leader case in `mvp_specification.md` §3.3), the same backfill-vs-degrade choice above applies, and civilization-designer's spec should state which one it expects so I don't have to guess. I am not proposing new fields myself — any need for fields the current spec doesn't have (e.g., birthplace, dynasty) should come from civilization-designer's proposal, at which point I'll extend the render templates to cover them.

**2. My role brief cites an LLM-usage boundary ("diplomacy wording, leader dialogue, historical summaries") that I could not find verbatim in `game_design_document.md`, `civ_identity_ambitions_specification.md`, `world_chronicle_specification.md`, or `emergent_crisis_history_specification.md`.** The GDD's actual, sourced boundary is coarser: LLMs are an Early Access-stage feature overall (§1), not itemized by which text surfaces get LLM treatment first. I've applied the coarser, sourced rule (no LLM in MVP, template-only) rather than assume the finer-grained split is accurate. If the finer split exists in a document I wasn't pointed to, the Project Director should reconcile which is authoritative — my recommendation should not be read as contradicting it, only as not finding a citation for it.

**3. No conflicting text observed from `atlas-civilization-designer` directly**, because as of this review that specialist's file did not yet exist in `docs/architecture/reviews/` (only `atlas-3d-architect.md`, `atlas-game-director.md`, and `atlas-world-designer.md` were present). The division proposed above is offered as a starting position for the Project Director to reconcile once civilization-designer's file lands, not as an agreed resolution.

Should the "diplomacy wording, leader dialogue, historical summaries" LLM-boundary phrasing be added to the GDD or a spec explicitly (in the §4-style addendum-note pattern `atlas-game-director` recommends), since it currently isn't sourced in any document I read but was given to me as project context?
